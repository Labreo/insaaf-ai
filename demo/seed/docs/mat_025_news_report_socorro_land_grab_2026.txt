Source: Northeast News, 'ED attaches 11 Goa plots worth Rs 4.2 crore in alleged land grab case', 24 September 2026 (ED, Panaji Zonal Office). Facts below are reconstructed from that public report for a demonstration; the client and plot details marked HYPOTHETICAL are illustrative, not court records.
The Enforcement Directorate provisionally attached 11 subdivided plots covering 4,086 square metres, plus a 914 square metre common area, within a 5,000 square metre land parcel at Socorro village, Bardez taluka, North Goa, valued at Rs 4.20 crore, under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002.
The land belonged to a deceased landowner. It was allegedly taken over using forged documents and a false identity claim that the father of two sisters living in Pune was the deceased owner.
The property was mutated in the sisters' names on the basis of a court order in inventory proceedings before a civil court.
On 17 February 2018 the sisters and their husbands executed a power of attorney over the property in favour of a Socorro resident.
A payment of Rs 9 lakh was made on 22 January 2018, before the power of attorney was executed and more than a year before the inventory proceedings.
The land was divided into 11 plots without planning permission and sold to nine buyers between 2018 and 2020 through brokers; the power-of-attorney holder executed the sale documents.
Five sale deeds of 2018 referred to inheritance through inventory proceedings that had not yet taken place.
The sale deeds recorded a combined consideration of Rs 63.65 lakh, while about Rs 1.49 crore was allegedly paid, much of it in cash and by cheques with blank payee details.
An FIR for cheating, impersonation and use of forged documents was registered by Porvorim police on a complaint from a legal heir of the original owner; the Special Investigation Team of the Goa Crime Branch is investigating.
